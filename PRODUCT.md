# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Broad Apple audience: curious readers who want a better home for articles, feeds, and bookmarks. Many are not power users yet; they arrive from search, social, or word of mouth and need to grasp what Cardmaniacs does in under a minute. A secondary segment includes experienced RSS and read-later users who compare tools, but the site should not assume that niche literacy.

Visitors are usually on a phone or Mac, often between tasks, evaluating whether this app deserves a download or a deeper read.

## Product Purpose

Cardmaniacs is a native Apple reading desk that unifies feeds, read-later, bookmarks, highlights, and imported documents into one calm command center. This website exists to explain that value clearly and earn App Store downloads without overselling.

Success looks like a visitor who understands the product (what it replaces, why it feels different) and takes the next step toward download. Editorial surfaces (blog, changelog, docs) support trust and retention, but conversion and comprehension are the primary goals.

## Brand Personality

Premium, native, crafted. Versatile and feature-rich without feeling dense: a power-user app that stays friendly to beginners. Voice is calm and confident, not hype-driven. The site should feel like the app on a desk: quiet chrome, editorial pacing, room to breathe.

Emotional goals: trust (this is a real, polished Apple-platform product), clarity (I know what this does), and relief (my reading life could be calmer).

Reference quality bar: Bear-like asymmetry, Things-like restraint, Craft-like surface craft, and Apple developer marketing's editorial confidence. Adapt composition and pacing from these; never copy palette, layout, or branding verbatim.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: hero metrics, identical icon-card grids, cream or sand body backgrounds, buzzword copy, conversion-template rhythm.
- Literal clones of Apple.com, Bear, Craft, or other reference apps.
- Dashboard or admin density on marketing pages.
- Playful startup illustration stacks that hide the real product UI.
- Dark terminal or hacker aesthetics (conflicts with the app's calm reading-desk identity).

## Design Principles

1. **Show the desk, not the pitch.** Lead with real app imagery, cropped reading surfaces, and product-specific chrome. The UI is the proof.
2. **Calm over conversion theater.** One clear primary CTA (App Store), restrained secondary paths, no metric-hero or urgency scaffolding.
3. **Power without intimidation.** Feature depth is visible, but hierarchy and copy welcome newcomers before rewarding experts.
4. **Native continuity.** Marketing surfaces should feel like the app extended to the web: same tokens, motion restraint, and editorial asymmetry.
5. **Adapt, don't mimic.** External references set a craft bar; Cardmaniacs colors, iconography, and screenshot art direction stay owned.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** for marketing pages and editorial content.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`: no content gated behind scroll reveals; motion enhances defaults that are already visible.
- Maintain readable contrast on frosted and tinted surfaces; body text must not wash out on ambient backgrounds.
- App Store CTAs stay honest when `PUBLIC_APP_STORE_URL` is unset (no fake download links).
